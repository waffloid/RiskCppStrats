\begin{abstract}
CRisky decomposes a real-time graph-strategy game into subproblems ---
building, transport, combat --- each attacked by specialized solvers rather
than end-to-end learning. This report makes that program empirical, and is
organized around four questions. \emph{Who wins?} A round-robin
Bradley--Terry ladder (symmetric duels, with a free-for-all as a second
environment; the two agree, $\rho = \ffaduelspearman$). \emph{How much can
any ranking capture?} Hodge decomposition of the pairwise structure:
\hodgetransitivityduel{} is transitive, and the cyclic remainder concentrates
in a single posture-organized cycle --- a rock-paper-scissors hypothesis
about its mechanism is tested with controlled archetypes and traced to a
game-design fact (standing armies are free). \emph{Are the component solvers
pulling their weight?} Gym rankings for economy, transport, and ordering,
read as diagnostics rather than objectives. \emph{What did benchmarking
teach us about the game itself?} A recurring set of mechanics --- early-war
decisiveness, free garrisons, load-dependent transit --- that the closing
section collects. Sweeping rankings are paired with single-game case
studies throughout, including a controlled counterfactual that isolates a
one-feature regression. All numbers and figures are generated artifacts,
reproducible from committed manifests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:method}

This report asks four questions, one per section. \S\ref{sec:elo}: which bot
is strongest, under a rating that is defensible? \S\ref{sec:transitivity}:
how much of this game can \emph{any} scalar rating capture --- and what
generates the part it cannot? \S\ref{sec:components}: within each engine
component (economy, transport, ordering, combat), which of the competing
solvers wins its gym? \S\ref{sec:findings}: collected findings --- about the
bots, about the game's mechanics, and about the benchmarks themselves ---
with the queued next steps.

Two habits run through all of it. First, every sweeping statistic is paired
with a zoom-in: single games, replayed by seed from the tournament logs, so
the anecdotes are drawn from the same population as the statistics. Second,
gym scores are treated as \emph{diagnostics}, never as the objective --- the
ladder is the only ground truth, and the places where a gym and the ladder
disagree turn out to be the most informative results in the report.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} The infrastructure is a three-way decomposition
at the repository root, each stratified by experiment:
\code{experiments/<name>/run.py} is the experiment definition (fixed solver
lists, seed bases, protocol; invoked as
\code{python3 -m experiments.<name>.run}, with companion \code{plot.py} and
analysis scripts that regenerate figures from raw data without re-running
games); \code{results/<name>/} holds the machine-produced artifacts
(\code{manifest.json} with git SHA, UTC date, exact command, and parameters;
\code{raw/*.csv} with every individual game; \code{summary.csv};
\code{tables/*.tex}; \code{figures/*.pdf}, all included by this document
directly); \code{writeup/} is this document. Reproducing any table or figure
is: check out the SHA in the manifest, build, re-run the module. This round
is a \emph{quick pass}: game counts give stable rankings in minutes, not
tight confidence intervals.

\section{Component benchmarks}
\label{sec:components}

Each engine component has competing solver proposals; each gets a gym, a
score, and a ranking. A reminder before the tables: these are
\emph{component} metrics, useful for ordering proposals within a subsystem
and for catching regressions cheaply --- they are not the objective. This
section contains two deliberate demonstrations of the gap: the economy gym's
best objective powers weaker bots than its worst-but-one
(\S\ref{sec:economy}), and the combat battery's control row shows the maps
themselves carry more signal than most matchups (\S\ref{sec:combat}).

\section{Findings and next steps}
\label{sec:findings}
\label{sec:open}

\paragraph{About the bots.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Build the missing bot.} \code{v8}'s distance softmax and
    \code{v12}'s OT transport are orthogonal strengths at the top of the
    ladder; their combination is unexplored (\S\ref{sec:elo-ladder}).
  \item \textbf{Diagnose \code{v11} before trusting demand netting.} The
    counterfactual of \S\ref{sec:elo-v11} isolates the regression to
    effective-troops netting; instrument the OT demands in a replayed
    stalling game to confirm the starvation mechanism.
  \item \textbf{Scale the ladder.} Four games per pair separates tiers, not
    neighbors --- the duel podium is an exact three-way tie. The harness is
    deterministic and parallel, and the Hodge/cycle analyses re-run on any
    extended log at no extra game cost.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{About the game.} Three mechanics surfaced as side effects of
benchmarking, and each suggests a design lever:
\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{3}
  \item \textbf{Standing armies are free}, so border-stacking dominates every
    other posture (\S\ref{sec:elo-rps}) and the frontline garrison decides
    ladder ranks and battery maps alike. Garrison upkeep --- or production
    diverted to supply --- would activate the posture rock-paper-scissors
    that is currently two-thirds real; an attack-suppression knob in
    ModelConfig would make it cleanly testable.
  \item \textbf{Large groups freeze} (speed $\propto 1/\text{count}$): the
    \code{path\_end} staircase (\S\ref{sec:transport}) is a blob crawling to
    a halt, the flows-over-time effect of \citet{koehler2002}. Confirm with
    \code{gym\_transport\_diag}; any transport solver that splits mass into
    convoys would exploit the mechanic rather than suffer it.
  \item \textbf{Games are decided in the first thousand ticks}: every case
    study is an early-war collapse, which is also why fast-payoff economies
    beat gym-optimal ones (\S\ref{sec:economy}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{About the benchmarks.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{6}
  \item \textbf{Symmetrize the combat battery} --- the \code{v4} self-play
    control shows a decisive player-1 map bias (\S\ref{sec:combat}); play
    both slots per matchup.
  \item \textbf{Replace the ordering metric} --- accumulated production
    cannot separate orderings (\S\ref{sec:ordering}); use the discounted
    objective $\int e^{-\lambda t}P(t)\,dt$.
  \item \textbf{Keep reading gym scores as diagnostics} --- the
    factory-biased paradox (\S\ref{sec:economy}) is the standing reminder.
\end{enumerate}
