\begin{abstract}
CRisky decomposes a real-time graph-strategy game into subproblems ---
building, transport, combat --- each attacked by specialized solvers rather
than end-to-end learning. This report makes that program empirical: every
component with competing proposals gets a benchmark, a score, and a ranking.
Bots are ranked by round-robin tournament under a Bradley--Terry model ---
symmetric 1v1 duels as the headline, a majority-hostile free-for-all as a
second environment (the two agree, $\rho = \ffaduelspearman$) --- and the
pairwise structure is Hodge-decomposed to measure how transitive the game
actually is (answer: \hodgetransitivityduel{} in duels --- the remaining
\hodgecurlduel{} is cyclic and invisible to any scalar rating). Economy,
transport, and ordering solvers are ranked in their gyms. Sweeping rankings
are paired with single-game case studies, including a controlled
counterfactual that isolates the \code{v11} regression. All numbers and
figures are generated artifacts, reproducible from committed manifests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Methodology and reproducibility}
\label{sec:method}

The empirical infrastructure is a three-way decomposition at the repository
root, each stratified by experiment:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{experiments/<name>/run.py} --- the experiment \emph{definition}:
    fixed solver lists, seed bases, protocol. Invoked as
    \code{python3 -m experiments.<name>.run}; companion \code{plot.py} and
    analysis scripts regenerate figures and derived tables from the raw data
    without re-running games.
  \item \code{results/<name>/} --- machine-produced artifacts:
    \code{manifest.json} (git SHA, UTC date, exact command, parameters),
    \code{raw/*.csv} (every individual game or run), \code{summary.csv},
    \code{tables/*.tex}, and \code{figures/*.pdf}, all of which this document
    includes directly.
  \item \code{writeup/} --- this document; one section per experiment.
\end{itemize}

Reproducing any table or figure is: check out the SHA in the manifest, build,
re-run the module. Case-study games replay tournament seeds, so the anecdotes
are drawn from the same population as the statistics. This round is a
\emph{quick pass}: game counts give stable rankings in minutes, not tight
confidence intervals; each section notes what a longer run would sharpen.

\section{Open items}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Build the missing bot.} \code{v8}'s distance softmax and
    \code{v12}'s OT transport are orthogonal and are ranks 1 and 3; their
    combination is unexplored (\S\ref{sec:elo-ladder}).
  \item \textbf{Diagnose \code{v11}.} The counterfactual of
    \S\ref{sec:elo-v11} isolates the regression to effective-troops demand
    netting; instrument the OT demands in a stalling game to confirm the
    starvation mechanism.
  \item \textbf{Scale the ladder.} Four games per pair separates tiers, not
    neighbors --- the duel podium is an exact three-way tie
    (\S\ref{sec:elo-ladder}). The harness is deterministic and parallel, and
    the Hodge analysis re-runs on any extended log at no extra game cost.
  \item \textbf{\code{path\_end} anomaly.} Both transport solvers plateau
    identically (Figure~\ref{fig:transport-curves}); point
    \code{gym\_transport\_diag} at it.
  \item \textbf{Ordering metric.} Accumulated production barely separates
    orderings (\S\ref{sec:ordering}); a discounted objective
    $\int e^{-\lambda t}P(t)\,dt$ would reward early payoff directly.
  \item \textbf{Symmetrize the combat battery.} The \code{v4} self-play
    control exposes a strong player-1 map bias (\S\ref{sec:combat}); play both
    slots per matchup.
  \item \textbf{Give garrisons an opportunity cost.} The RPS posture cycle
    fails at exactly one edge --- scissors never out-builds paper --- because
    parked troops are free (\S\ref{sec:elo-cycles}). Upkeep or
    production-diversion would make the posture wheel live; an
    attack-suppression knob in ModelConfig would make it cleanly testable.
\end{enumerate}
