\subsection{Protocol}

Fix an economy plan (MCMC-annealed layouts on 10 seeded graphs), then choose
the \emph{order} in which to execute its build steps, paying each structure's
cost from accumulated production. Scores: \emph{accumulated production} (the
integral of the production curve over the build-out; rewards early payoff)
and \emph{ticks to complete} the plan.

Accumulated production is nearly flat across orderings (spread
$\approx 0.05\%$, far below the seed-to-seed sd) --- on these plans the metric
is dominated by the plan itself, not its ordering. Ticks-to-complete
discriminates sharply: \code{cheapest\_first} finishes 36\% sooner than naive
\code{sequential} (2160 vs.\ 3379 ticks), with \code{production\_gradient}
--- greedily building whichever remaining step most increases production ---
second on completion (2792) while edging the accumulation metric.

Verdict: \code{cheapest\_first} to get the plan online fast,
\code{production\_gradient} to front-load the production curve. The headline
metric cannot separate them further; a discounted objective
$\int e^{-\lambda t} P(t)\,dt$, which directly encodes the design notes'
argument for average-time optimization, is the proposed fix
(\S\ref{sec:open}, item 5).
