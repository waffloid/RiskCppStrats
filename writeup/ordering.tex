\textbf{Benchmark.} Fix an economy plan (MCMC-annealed layouts on 10 seeded
graphs), then choose the \emph{order} in which to execute its build steps,
paying each structure's cost from accumulated production. Scores:
\emph{accumulated production} (the integral of the production curve over the
build-out; higher rewards early payoff) and \emph{ticks to complete} the plan.

\textbf{Reading.} Accumulated production is nearly flat across orderings
(spread $\approx 0.05\%$, far under the seed-to-seed sd) --- on these plans the
metric is dominated by the plan itself, not its ordering. Ticks-to-complete
discriminates sharply: \code{cheapest\_first} finishes 36\% sooner than naive
\code{sequential} (2160 vs.\ 3379 ticks), with \code{production\_gradient} ---
greedily building whichever remaining step most increases production --- second
(2792) while edging the accumulation metric. Verdict: \code{cheapest\_first}
if you want the plan online fast, \code{production\_gradient} if you want the
production curve front-loaded; the current headline metric cannot separate them
further, motivating the discounted objective proposed in \S\ref{sec:open}.
