\subsection{Combat battery}
\label{sec:combat}

\paragraph{Protocol.} Pure tactics: building is disabled and each candidate
model fights a fixed baseline (\code{v4}) on four hand-built maps ---
\code{corridor} (a path with opposing factory chains), \code{bipartite\_3\_5}
(a dense frontier), \code{star\_hub} (one side holds the hub), and
\code{grid\_5x5} (diagonal split). This isolates the war agents and
transport from economy and expansion, which dominate the ladder of
\S\ref{sec:elo}. Scores: wins over all battery runs and aggregate kill/death
ratio. \code{v4} appears against itself as a control row.

\paragraph{Reading.} The control row dominates the interpretation: \code{v4}
against itself wins 0/12 as player~0, i.e.\ these maps are heavily biased
toward the player-1 slot, and raw win counts must be read as \emph{wins
above control}, not win rates. By that standard \code{v12} is the only model
that beats the asymmetry anywhere --- it sweeps \code{corridor} 3/3, exactly
the map its frontline-garrison pooling was built for. This is the garrison's
third appearance as the decisive ingredient (after the \code{v11}
counterfactual of \S\ref{sec:elo-v11} and the paper-dominance result of
\S\ref{sec:elo-rps}): under the current rules, holding massed troops at the
border is simply the best thing a bot can do with them. The K/D column adds
texture: \code{v1\_knapsack} trades at 5.3:1 yet only stalemates (3 draws)
--- efficient attrition without the transport to convert it --- while
\code{v9}'s OT transport alone (0.65~K/D, matching the control) does not
help on pure-combat maps. Playing both slots per matchup is the cheap fix
(\S\ref{sec:open}).

\paragraph{Case study: anatomy of a corridor win.}
Figure~\ref{fig:corridor} shows per-tick telemetry of a \code{v12} corridor
victory, i.e.\ \emph{how} the garrison wins. The two panels tell one story
from two sides. Troop totals (left): both models' production tracks
identically until $\sim$tick 400, then \code{v4}'s curve turns into a
sawtooth --- it repeatedly masses a stack, attacks, and loses it --- while
\code{v12}'s keeps compounding, because its pooled frontline garrison makes
each defensive exchange favorable. Cumulative kills (right): \code{v12}'s
kills climb in discrete staircase jumps, one per repelled \code{v4} assault,
reaching a $\sim$4:1 final margin. The battle is decided not by one
breakthrough but by \code{v4} funding \code{v12}'s kill count in
installments; the final collapse after tick 2600 is \code{v4}'s last stack
breaking against the garrison.
