\textbf{Benchmark.} Pure tactics: building is disabled and each candidate
model fights a fixed baseline (\code{v4}) on four hand-built maps ---
\code{corridor} (a path with opposing factory chains), \code{bipartite\_3\_5}
(a dense frontier), \code{star\_hub} (one side holds the hub), and
\code{grid\_5x5} (diagonal split). This isolates the war agents and transport
from economy and expansion, which dominate the full-game ladder of
\S\ref{sec:elo}. Scores: wins over all benchmark runs and aggregate kill/death
ratio.

\textbf{Reading.} The control row dominates the interpretation: \code{v4}
against itself wins 0/12 as player~0, i.e.\ these maps are heavily biased
toward the player-1 slot, and raw win counts must be read as \emph{wins above
control}, not win rates. By that standard \code{v12} is the only model that
beats the asymmetry anywhere --- it sweeps \code{corridor} 3/3, which is
exactly the map its frontline-garrison pooling was built for. The K/D column
adds texture the win column misses: \code{v1\_knapsack} trades at 5.3:1 yet
only stalemates (3 draws) --- efficient attrition without the transport to
convert it --- while \code{v9}'s OT transport alone (0.65 K/D, matching the
\code{v4} control) does not help on pure-combat maps. A symmetric protocol
(each model playing both slots) is the cheap fix for the position bias.
