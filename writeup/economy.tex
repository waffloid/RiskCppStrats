\subsection{Economy solvers}
\label{sec:economy}

\paragraph{Protocol.} The player owns an entire Poisson-generated map with
ample troops on every node; a solver proposes a factory/powerplant layout;
the score is \emph{efficiency}: achieved production rate divided by a
(loose, generally infeasible) upper bound that assumes every node is a
factory with all neighbors powerplants. The placement problem is a signed
quadratic assignment on the graph --- a MAX-CUT variant \citep{goemans1995},
encoded in Ising form \citep{lucas2014} and solved by simulated annealing
\citep{kirkpatrick1983,metropolis1953} from a greedy warm start, or by
Goemans--Williamson-style SDP rounding \citep{goemans1995}. Ten graphs per
size, seeds fixed in the manifest.

\paragraph{Reading.} Three tiers, cleanly separated at every seed
(Figure~\ref{fig:economy}): the annealed QUBO variants and MCMC are
statistically indistinguishable from one another ($\approx 0.38$--$0.39$);
the greedy local rule reaches less than half of that; the bootstrap opening
book (four factories, one powerplant, by design) does not compete on this
metric. Within the top tier, the choice of QUBO objective moves the mean by
less than one standard deviation --- objective engineering is not where the
headroom is.

Two caveats, and the second is this report's clearest example of the proxy
principle. First, the denominator is a relaxation, so 0.39 is \emph{not}
``39\% of what is achievable''; relative order is the signal. Second, the
factory-biased objective sits measurably below the other QUBO variants here
yet powers the stronger \emph{bots} (v6 and every OT-era model). The
resolution is the early-war-collapse pattern of \S\ref{sec:elo-podium}:
games are decided in the first thousand ticks, and factories pay off before
their powerplant neighborhoods complete --- a dynamic a static, infinite-
horizon efficiency benchmark cannot see.
