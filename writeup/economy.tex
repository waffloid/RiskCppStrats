\textbf{Benchmark.} The player owns an entire Poisson-generated map with ample
troops on every node; a solver proposes a factory/powerplant layout; the score
is \emph{efficiency}: achieved production rate divided by a (loose, generally
infeasible) upper bound that assumes every node is a factory with all neighbors
powerplants. The placement problem is a signed quadratic assignment on the graph
--- a MAX-CUT variant \citep{goemans1995}, encoded in Ising form
\citep{lucas2014} and solved by simulated annealing
\citep{kirkpatrick1983,metropolis1953} from a greedy warm start, or by
Goemans--Williamson-style SDP rounding \citep{goemans1995}. Ten graphs per
size, seeds fixed in the manifest.

\textbf{Reading.} The annealed QUBO variants are statistically
indistinguishable from one another ($\approx 0.39$) and a hair above raw MCMC
over node assignments; both roughly double the greedy local rule, and the
bootstrap opening-book (four factories and one powerplant, by design) is not a
contender on this metric. Two caveats. First, the denominator is a relaxation,
so 0.39 is \emph{not} ``39\% of what is achievable''; relative order is the
signal. Second, the factory-biased objective ranks last among QUBO variants
here yet produces stronger \emph{bots} (v6 and every OT-era model use it) ---
in-game, factories pay off before their powerplant neighborhoods complete, a
dynamic this static benchmark cannot see. Gym efficiency is a diagnostic, not
the objective.
